    Rules to remember

    • Equity needed = your call ÷ the pot after you call. Never compare the call with the pot before the bet.
    • The two-card (Rule of 4) figure applies only when someone is all-in. With stacks behind, compare the one-card odds with the current price.
    • Count only outs that win. Discount draws to a second-best hand, outs that complete a flush for someone else, and pairing your ace against a set.
    • Implied odds need deep effective stacks and a hidden, strong hand. Set mining wants at least 15× the raise behind, about 25× out of position.
    • Reverse implied odds: if you won't be sure to have the best hand when you hit, count fewer outs and expect to win less.
    • EV(call) = equity × the pot you win − (1 − equity) × your call. EV(bluff) = fold% × pot − call% × bet.
    • MDF is a sanity check. Against players who rarely bluff, fold more.
    • Short stacks kill speculative hands: at 25bb, set mining is pointless, and high-card hands like K7s gain. In tournaments, play all-in or fold at about 10–15bb or less, mix shoves with small raises up to about 20bb, and min-raise above that.
    • SPR = effective stack ÷ pot on the flop. About 17.7 after a called button open, about 4 in a called 3-bet pot, about 1.6 in a called 4-bet pot.

    Read the flop

    Ask four things about every flop, in this order. The first one that fits names the texture.

    1. Paired? A paired flop misses almost every range. Bet often and small.
    2. One suit? A monotone flop cuts betting frequency and size sharply.
    3. Low and connected? Three cards 9 or lower that fit inside five ranks make straights possible. These flops favour the caller.
    4. How high, how connected? A rainbow A-, K- or Q-high flop with no draws keeps the raiser's edge. A J- or 10-high flop with connected or two-tone cards gives the caller draws.

    Two kinds of advantage

    Range advantage (more total equity) decides how often you bet. Nut advantage (more of the very best hands) decides how big.

    Bet for a reason

    Before betting, name which worse hands call or which better hands fold. If neither group is meaningful, check.

    Continuation bets by board and situation

    A beginner default built from Upswing and GTO Wizard. Size: 25–35% pot on dry, static boards; 55–80% on wet, dynamic ones; 66% or more when you bet the turn again; 25–40% in 3-bet pots.

    Four player types

    Label the opponent first. The adjustment follows from the label.

      Rules to remember

      • Label the opponent first: how many hands they play, how often they raise, and whether they bluff.
      • Calling stations: value bet big and often, thin hands included, and never bluff.
      • Nits: steal their blinds and c-bet to make them fold, but believe them when they bet or raise.
      • Loose-aggressive players: let them bluff. Check-raise and call down with your strong hands, and re-raise them preflop.
      • Tight-aggressive players: respect their raises, value bet your good hands, and skip the marginal spots.
      • Against passive players, bet your strong hands yourself instead of slowplaying to induce bluffs.
      • Big river bets at low stakes are mostly value. Unless you beat a good part of their value range, fold.
      • River raises at these limits are almost always value, and against typical opponents you can fold about 47% to a flop check-raise.
      • Before you bet, name which worse hands call or which better hands fold. If neither group is meaningful, check.

          Your decisions by chapter

          The routine, street by street

          The guide's worked hand ties the four chapters together. Run the same checks on every hand.

          1. Preflop: your seat and the action decide the range. Raise first in from the chart, never limp, and price calls against raises with the effective stack.
          2. Flop: name the texture and whose range it favours, then sort your hand into a bucket. Check the stack-to-pot ratio before you plan to commit.
          3. Facing a bet: divide the call by the pot after you call, and count only the outs that actually win.
          4. Every street: label the opponent and adjust. Value bet calling stations, steal from nits, let loose-aggressive players bluff, and believe big river bets from passive players.
          5. Before any bet: name which worse hands call or which better hands fold. If neither group is meaningful, check.
